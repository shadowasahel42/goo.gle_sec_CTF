About the event

In the 2050s, cyber insurance nearly collapsed because no one could agree on what had actually happened after a breach. The solution was the Clearinghouse: an independent body that freezes a victim’s environment, preserves the record, and certifies the reconstruction of an incident.

On October 29, enter the Cymbal Investments Cold Room. Three incidents unfolded across the same environment in eight days. A marketing click that should have meant nothing. A trusted developer tool doing work nobody asked it to do. An account that belonged to someone else for about an hour.

They look unrelated. Your assignment is to prove what happened.

Working inside the frozen environment, you’ll follow evidence across identity, endpoints, cloud activity, mail, build systems, and data. You’ll need to distinguish malicious actions from legitimate maintenance happening at almost the same time, reconstruct each intrusion end to end, and file a defensible finding before the competing reconstructors beat you to the record.

And somewhere in the evidence may be something the Clearinghouse did not ask you to find: the Tell, a detail that could connect one operator to far more than a single incident.

Can you reconstruct the truth before someone else defines it?

Methodology

All three incidents happened on 15 October 2026. I figured the fastest way to work inside a frozen environment was to live in UDM search in Google SecOps, with the time range set to LAST 2 MONTHS so nothing fell outside the window.

Lessons learned

  • The answer lives in the Raw Log. The UI truncates data.
  • Pivot on the right identifier: Workspace = email, Sysmon = Windows user, Proxy = username. Use the grouped fields.
  • Injected processes run as SYSTEM, so search by hostname.
  • Watch UDM syntax: regex goes in slashes, and underscores must be exact.
